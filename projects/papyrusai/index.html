Teacher-in-the-loop scoring in PapyrusAI

Bringing multi-agent essay scoring into a classroom writing coach, with teachers in control.

PapyrusAI is the Digital Learning Lab’s generative-AI writing environment. Students work through planning, drafting, revising and reflecting in guided conversations that start from research-based prompts; instructors decide which prompts are available and can modify them, and the design principle is to support students without doing the work for them. The platform was built by a UCI team with National Science Foundation support and is now being developed and evaluated by the IES-funded WRITE AI Center.

Automated scores only help a classroom if teachers can check and correct them before students see them. In a CSCL 2026 paper I proposed a teacher-in-the-loop multi-agent framework for transparent essay scoring and feedback: agents score each rubric dimension and explain their reasoning, and teachers review, edit and approve scores and feedback before anything is released to students (Keramati & Warschauer, 2026).

Within PapyrusAI and the WRITE AI Center, I work on essay scoring and feedback that builds on the multi-agent debate method from MADRAG (Keramati et al., 2026), and on evaluating the AI models and prompts behind the platform. The public project page describes the feature as Multi-Agent Review: “A multi-agent system provides scoring and feedback on student writing, in alignment with instructor rubrics.”

The scorer’s validation against human ratings is preregistered on the Open Science Framework: the debate-scorer evaluation on public essay sets (osf.io/7qpwg) and, with Tamara Tate, a human–AI agreement study on authentic essays from undergraduate writing-intensive courses (osf.io/5c9nk). I also contributed to a longitudinal study of AI–human score agreement in primary-grade writing, presented at the NCME AIME-Con 2026 conference.

References

2026

  1. CSCL
    A Teacher-in-the-Loop Multi-Agent Framework for Transparent Essay Scoring and Feedback
    Ali Keramati and Mark Warschauer
    In Proceedings of the 19th International Conference on Computer-Supported Collaborative Learning (CSCL 2026), Jun 2026
    Abs DOI Bib PDF

    Proposes a teacher-in-the-loop multi-agent framework for essay scoring and feedback: a supervisor agent routes each rubric trait to specialized worker agents that score it and explain their reasoning, while the teacher monitors, reviews, and curates the scores and feedback that students receive.

  2. NLP4DH
    MADRAG: Multi-Agent Debate with Retrieval-Augmented Generation for Training-Free Analytic Essay Scoring
    Ali Keramati, Shiyuan Zhou, Sharad Mehrotra, and Mark Warschauer
    In Proceedings of the 6th International Conference on Natural Language Processing for the Digital Humanities, Jul 2026
    Abs DOI arXiv Bib PDF

    Presents MADRAG, a training-free analytic essay scoring framework that combines multi-agent debate with retrieval-augmented exemplar grounding. The system improves calibration, reduces middle-score bias, and achieves strong trait-level scoring performance that is competitive with supervised essay scoring approaches.
