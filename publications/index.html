publications

publications in reverse chronological order.

2026

  1. The First Tokens Matter: Early Confidence Signals for Evaluating LLM Reasoning
    2026
    Submitted to ICLR 2026 (under review)
    Abs HTML

    Examines whether early token-level confidence signals can predict the reasoning quality of large language models in multi-agent debate systems. The findings show that signals from the first few generated tokens are especially informative for estimating reasoning reliability, offering a lightweight way to monitor and evaluate multi-agent LLM performance.

  2. NLP4DH
    MADRAG: Multi-Agent Debate with Retrieval-Augmented Generation for Training-Free Analytic Essay Scoring
    Ali Keramati, Shiyuan Zhou, Sharad Mehrotra, and Mark Warschauer
    In Proceedings of the 6th International Conference on Natural Language Processing for the Digital Humanities, 2026
    Abs DOI arXiv Bib

    Presents MADRAG, a training-free analytic essay scoring framework that combines multi-agent debate with retrieval-augmented exemplar grounding. The system improves calibration, reduces middle-score bias, and achieves strong trait-level scoring performance that is competitive with supervised essay scoring approaches.

  3. ACL SRW
    The Confident Liar: Diagnosing Multi-Agent Debate with Log-Probabilities and LLM-as-Judge
    Ali Keramati, Justin Cheok, Jacob Horne, and Mark Warschauer
    In Proceedings of the 64th Annual Meeting of the Association for Computational Linguistics (Volume 4: Student Research Workshop), 2026
    Abs DOI arXiv Bib

    Diagnoses failure modes in multi-agent debate systems by combining token-level log-probability signals with LLM-as-judge evaluation. The work shows how confidence calibration can surface cases where agents are confidently wrong, improving the reliability of multi-agent reasoning for evaluation tasks.

  4. EDM
    Simulating Students’ Java Programming Errors with Large Language Models
    Ali Keramati, Jie Cao, Iman Mohammadi, Mark Warschauer, and Yang Shi
    In Proceedings of the International Conference on Educational Data Mining (EDM 2026), 2026
    Abs arXiv Bib

    Investigates whether large language models can realistically simulate the programming errors that students make when learning Java. By generating authentic error patterns, the work supports the development of educational tools, automated tutoring, and large-scale evaluation of programming-education interventions.

  5. LAK
    Disagreement as Data: Reasoning Trace Analytics in Multi-Agent Systems
    Elham Tajik, Conrad Borchers, Bahar Shahrokhian, Sebastian Simon, Ali Keramati, Sonika Pal, and Sreecharan Sankaranarayanan
    In Proceedings of the 16th International Learning Analytics and Knowledge Conference (LAK26), 2026
    Abs DOI arXiv Bib

    Proposes a new way to analyze large language model reasoning traces in multi-agent systems by treating disagreement as a meaningful analytic signal. The work shows how semantic similarity between agent reasoning can help detect coding ambiguity, improve qualitative analysis workflows, and strengthen human–AI collaboration in educational research.

  6. ICLS
    Mapping Instructors’ Attitudes Towards Cheating with AI: An Exploratory Analysis Using Forum Analysis
    Iman Mohammadi, Ali Keramati, and Sina Rismanchian
    In Proceedings of the 20th International Conference of the Learning Sciences (ICLS 2026), 2026
    Abs DOI Bib

    Explores how instructors perceive and respond to AI-assisted cheating through an exploratory analysis of online forum discussions. The work surfaces the range of attitudes, concerns, and proposed responses among educators as generative AI becomes widespread in academic settings.

2025

  1. NeurIPS
    Application of Multi-Agent Systems for Essay Scoring
    Ali Keramati and Mark Warschauer
    In NeurIPS 2025 Education Program, 2025
    Contributed talk (oral)
    Abs Bib HTML Website

    Introduces multi-agent system architectures for essay scoring, comparing approaches such as supervisor, collaboration, and debate. The work highlights how multi-agent debate can improve scoring reliability and move automated essay evaluation closer to human-level agreement while making advanced AI concepts more accessible for educators and assessment professionals.

  2. Poster
    MADEST: Multi-Agent Debate Essay Scoring Triangulation
    Ali Keramati and Mark Warschauer
    2025
    First-year Ph.D. poster
    Abs DOI Bib HTML

    A novel approach to automated essay scoring using multi-agent systems. By leveraging debate between specialized agents, the framework provides a more reliable and nuanced evaluation, surpassing traditional single-agent methods and bringing scoring closer to human-level agreement.

  3. CSICC
    iTAG: Easy, Rapid, Automatic Intelligent Tagging for Educational Contents
    Narges Shahhoseini, Fattaneh Taghiyareh, and Ali Keramati
    In 2025 29th International Computer Conference, Computer Society of Iran (CSICC), 2025
    Abs DOI Bib

    Automatic metadata tagging of educational content to enhance Intelligent Tutoring Systems (ITS). Leveraging NLP, machine learning, and domain-specific rules, it efficiently extracts both general and pedagogical metadata, improving personalized learning recommendations.

2024

  1. ICCKE
    Automating Theory of Mind Assessment with a LLaMA-3-Powered Chatbot: Enhancing Faux Pas Detection in Autism
    Avisa Fallah, Ali Keramati, Mohammad Ali Nazari, and Fatemeh Sadat Mirfazeli
    In 2024 14th International Conference on Computer and Knowledge Engineering (ICCKE), 2024
    Abs DOI Bib

    Implemented Theory of Mind assessment for individuals with Autism by developing a LLaMA-3 chatbot to administer the Faux Pas Recognition Test. The system utilizes a multi-phase pipeline that combines interactive rehabilitation dialogues with adaptive hints, an automated scoring phase using an LLM-as-judge, and generates clinician-ready reports to enhance social cognition and improve faux pas detection.

  2. arXiv
    AI-powered Digital Framework for Personalized Economical Quality Learning at Scale
    Mrzieh VatandoustMohammadieh, Mohammad Mahdi Mohajeri, Ali Keramati, and Majid Nili Ahmadabadi
    2024
    Abs arXiv Bib

    AI-powered digital learning framework designed for scalable, personalized, and cost-effective education. Framework integrates AI-based learner modeling, a personalized recommender system, and AI-assisted support for both learners and facilitators, aiming to address challenges in educational access, soft skills development, and large-scale implementation.

  3. Journal
    Students’ Reflection on the Effect of Collaborative Learning on the Learning Environment and Academic Achievement in Online Reflective Platforms
    Ali Keramati, Mohammad Reza Keramati, and Mohammad Hossein Arefian
    Reflective Practice, 2024
    Abs DOI Bib

    Explores the impact of collaborative learning (CL) on the learning environment and academic achievement in online reflective platforms (ORPs). The mixed-methods approach reveals that CL enhances student engagement, promotes a supportive learning atmosphere, and improves academic performance, with positive reflections from students on the collaborative and interactive nature of the online learning experience.

  4. Journal
    Challenges and Solutions of Implementing Cooperative Learning in Classroom Environments: Lived Experiences of Primary School Teachers in Iran
    Mohammad Reza Keramati, Robyn M. Gillies, Fahimeh Ansarizadeh, and Ali Keramati
    International Journal of Early Years Education, 2024
    Abs DOI Bib

    Identifies challenges in implementing cooperative learning (CL) in Iran’s primary schools, including traditional teaching methods, stakeholder readiness, and lack of facilities. Solutions include improving curriculum, fostering positive attitudes, and enhancing teacher training for effective CL implementation.
